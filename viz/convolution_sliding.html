A convolution layer slides one small kernel of learned weights across its input, and at every stop it computes a single multiply-accumulate: each overlapped input value is multiplied by the aligned weight and the products are summed into one output number. 1-D treats a raw echo waveform (1×8) as a time series and slides a 3-tap kernel along time. 2-D treats a spectrogram patch (6×6) as an image and slides a 3×3 kernel over time and frequency at once. Switch modes with the 1-D/2-D buttons; play animates the slide, prev/next and the position slider step through every output cell, and the equation shows exactly which products form the highlighted result. Both kernels here are fixed edge detectors so the arithmetic stays checkable — in a trained network these weights are what learning adjusts. Keys: space play/pause, ←/→ step.

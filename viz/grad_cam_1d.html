Grad-CAM on a small 1D convolutional network that reads raw audio. A 1024-sample waveform holds a click, a tone and a rising chirp. Two strided conv layers turn it into 6 channels over 64 time steps; Grad-CAM then backpropagates the chosen class logit to those channels, averages each gradient row over time into a weight, sums the weighted channels, keeps the positive part and interpolates it back onto the samples. Because the last conv layer has only a time axis, the map says when the evidence occurs, at 16-sample resolution. Step through with the buttons or arrow keys, switch the class to see the highlight move, or remove an event to watch the map latch onto whatever weakly resembles it. On the conv step, move the pointer over the diagram to follow one time step's receptive field; steps 1 to 7 loop an animation of their operation, which the pause button stops. Filters are hand-set band-pass, sweep and onset detectors, not trained weights; every number is computed live.
